The Peer Review examines how the MIM ran the response. The [After Action](/governance/after-action/) examines what happened to the incident. They are complementary, not duplicates.

A technically successful incident can still have a poor Peer Review score. A technically complex incident that nearly failed can have an excellent one. The Peer Review is about command quality — not outcomes.

---

## What a Peer Review Is

A structured quality assessment of a MIM's incident command, completed by a second certified MIM after incident close. It covers eight competency domains. It produces a locked record attached to the incident.

Peer Reviews are not disciplinary. They are developmental. The data feeds the MIM's rolling Command Score, which informs certification renewal and development conversations.

---

## The Eight Competency Domains

### 1. Command Presence
Was the MIM in control of the bridge from declaration to close? Did they hold the floor clearly, redirect noise without disrupting cadence, and project confidence appropriate to the severity?

### 2. Phase Discipline
Were phase transitions deliberate and timely? Were any phases skipped, rushed, or held longer than warranted by conditions? Was the Validation phase respected before Resolution was called?

### 3. Air Time Discipline
Was CAN format enforced? Were open questions tracked and resolved? Was speculation labeled as such? Did the bridge maintain signal-to-noise throughout?

### 4. Escalation Judgment
Were alarm level changes timely and appropriately calibrated? Was the team paged when conditions warranted it, or was there identifiable hesitation? Were de-escalation decisions logged and communicated?

### 5. Stakeholder Communication
Were milestones published on the committed cadence? Was the "next update at" commitment maintained? Were executive-level communications proportionate and accurate?

### 6. Recovery Track Management
Were tracks opened with named owners and timeboxes? Were stalled tracks identified and redirected? Was the timebox discipline maintained throughout Isolation and Mitigation?

### 7. Incident Record Quality
Is the incident record complete enough to reconstruct the timeline? Are the milestones adequate to support the Learning Review draft? Would a MIM who was not on the call be able to understand what happened from the record alone?

### 8. Close-Out Readiness
Was resolution called with full Validation completed? Was the After Action scheduled before the bridge closed? Was the command team formally released?

## Who Conducts the Peer Review

A second certified MIM — not the reviewing MIM's manager, not someone who was on the bridge in a command role during the incident.

The reviewer reads the incident record, listens to a bridge recording if available, and completes the Peer Review form within 5 business days of incident close.

---

## Scoring

Each domain is scored on a 1–4 scale:

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 4 | Exemplary — could be used as a training example |
| 3 | Meets standard — competent, no significant gaps |
| 2 | Developing — identifiable gaps that affected the incident |
| 1 | Below standard — significant gaps with probable incident impact |

The overall score is not an average. The reviewer provides a narrative assessment and highlights the highest-impact domains — positive and negative.

---

## The Command Score

Each MIM's Peer Reviews accumulate into a rolling **Command Score** — a visibility tool, not a ranking system. The Command Score is:

- Visible to the MIM and their manager
- Used in certification renewal conversations
- Not used as a performance review metric in isolation

A declining Command Score triggers a development conversation. A consistently strong Command Score supports progression to senior MIM or trainer designation.

---

## The Peer Review Record

The Peer Review produces a locked `PR-{number}` record attached to the incident. It includes:

- Reviewer identity and certification level
- Domain scores with narrative notes
- One "what went well" highlight per review (required)
- One "area for development" highlight per review (required)
- Overall narrative assessment

The record is locked 48 hours after the reviewer submits it. It cannot be edited after locking — it is an auditable record of command quality at a point in time.

## Relationship to After Action

| | After Action | Peer Review |
|---|---|---|
| **Examines** | The incident — what happened, root cause, improvement actions | The command — how the MIM ran the response |
| **Participants** | MIM + technical team + SMEs | One additional certified MIM |
| **Output** | Learning Review document + action items | Locked PR record + Command Score input |
| **Cadence** | After every Critical and qualifying High | After every Major Incident a certified MIM runs |
| **Public to** | All incident participants + management | MIM + their manager |
